Overview

AeroGlass Window Film™ is a peel-and-stick upgrade for windows. It is meant to save energy, protect interiors, and clean the air when sunlight reaches it.

It pairs a heat-reflecting layer with a coating of titanium dioxide (TiO₂) and graphene. In everyday terms: a catalyst that uses light to break down smog ingredients (nitrogen oxides, volatile organic compounds and other organic pollutants), while the film lowers heating and cooling bills.

How to use this page. Every number carries a status label, so you can tell a goal from a proven result (see Verification protocol). Use the contents list to jump around. Nothing you do here is stored or sent anywhere.

How it works

Think of the film as four jobs happening in order as sunlight arrives:

  1. Solar-selective layer. Reflects infrared (heat) while letting visible light through, so rooms stay bright but cooler.
  2. Photocatalytic nanocoating (TiO₂ + graphene). Uses light to break down NOx, VOCs and other organic pollutants that touch it.
  3. Thermal buffer. A thin insulating layer that slows heat loss through the glass.
  4. Self-cleaning surface. Reduces dust and grime build-up, so there is little to maintain.

Verification protocol

Each performance claim is a falsifiable hypothesis with an acceptance rule agreed before testing. Register the rules, then publish results whether or not they succeed.

H4 Energy savings20–35% less heating and cooling energyIPMVP Option B or C with matched control zones and degree-day normalisation. Judge model fit with ASHRAE Guideline 14 (CV(RMSE), NMBE).Accept if the savings interval excludes zero and the model fit passes. Report by climate and orientation.

Treat the decision rules as starting points and agree them with your evaluator first. Standard photocatalysis tests use UV-A lamps, but light behind glass is weaker and filtered, so a standard-test result alone does not transfer to a window.

A Monte Carlo model (4,000 runs, in your browser) estimates net present value (NPV) over the film’s life. Honest questions

The film blocks UV. How can its coating still clean air?

TiO₂ is mainly switched on by ultraviolet light, and window glass already filters much of it. Graphene blends are studied as a way to respond to visible light too. Whether that is strong enough behind glass is exactly what Phase 1 testing must show.

Will it make my home darker, or colder in winter?

The aim is to keep visible light clear. Any heat-reflecting film can reduce free winter sun, so the net winter effect depends on your climate. That is why savings are shown as a range.

What happens to the pollutants it breaks down?

The goal is simple, harmless compounds. Partial breakdown can leave other compounds behind, so by-product testing comes before any air-cleaning claim.

Is it safe on all windows?

Films can add heat stress to some double-pane or low-E glass. Check your window maker’s guidance before installing.
